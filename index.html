A VERIFICATION BREAKTHROUGH

A transcript is just text.
A sealed trace is proof.

Anyone can type a transcript, edit one, screenshot one. That's why “here's what the AI said” does not count.

The insight: your session already runs over an encrypted, authenticated connection to the AI provider. The protocol relays that session through an independent notary that witnesses exactly what crossed the wire without ever being able to read it. Old cryptography, pointed at a new problem.

TRUST MODEL

A SEALED TRACE PROVES

  • ✓The disclosed bytes traveled an authenticated connection with the named AI provider.
  • ✓They have not changed since capture.
  • ✓The named notary witnessed them at the stated time.
  • ✓With live capture enabled: the exact timeline as it actually unfolded.

IT CANNOT PROVE

  • ✕That every interaction was disclosed.
  • ✕Who was behind the keyboard.
  • ✕That no other AI or device was used.
  • ✕That an output was correct, lawful, or wise.

Exalto Seal is not a notary public. Sealing is not a notarial act. A receipt is cryptographic evidence, not a legal instrument. The ENP specification uses "notary" as a technical role term, in the way public-key infrastructure uses "certificate authority."
